01 — Features

What it does, plainly.

01

Automatic history

Every copy is captured, newest on top. Re-copying bumps an item up instead of duplicating it — rapid back-to-back copies are never dropped, unlike Win+V.

02

Text and images

Both live in the same list. Image clips show thumbnails; oversized dumps are skipped so the history stays fast.

03

Search that keeps up

A pill search bar filters as you type. Press / to jump to it, Enter to copy the top match.

04

Pins

Passwords, snippets, links — pinned items stay on top and survive restarts and Clear.

05

One-click copy-back

Click any clip and it's on the clipboard, flashing green. Paste anywhere.

06

Out of the way

A small floating button that stays on top, a tray menu (pause monitoring, launch at login, history size 25–200), and a global shortcut that works from any app.

02 — How it works

Copy, tap, paste.

  1. 1

    Copy like always

    Nothing changes about how you work. ClipTap files each copy away in the background.

  2. 2

    Tap to open

    Ctrl+Shift+V or the floating button. Search, pin, or delete.

  3. 3

    Click to paste back

    One click returns it to the clipboard. Then paste wherever you were.

05 — FAQ

Fair questions.

Cost and license

Free, MIT licensed. The source, the issue tracker, and the releases are all on GitHub.

Platforms

Windows only — installer and portable builds. It's an Electron app with plain HTML/CSS/JS renderers.

Where does my data go

Nowhere. Clips live on your disk under %APPDATA%\ClipTap\clips\. The app makes zero network requests; monitoring can be paused from the tray.

Why not just use Win+V

No search, no pins, no organization — and it silently drops rapid consecutive copies. That gap is the entire reason ClipTap exists.

It broke. Now what

Open %APPDATA%\ClipTap\ClipTap.log (or tray → Open log file) and attach the last lines to a GitHub issue.
